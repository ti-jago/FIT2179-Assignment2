Section 01

Where Visitors Come From

Australia's inbound tourism is dominated by its Asia-Pacific neighbours. New Zealand leads in trip volume, driven by the ease of trans-Tasman travel, while China and India represent the largest long-haul markets. Europe and the Americas, though smaller in volume, tend to stay longer and spend more per trip.

Section 02

Economic Value of Each Market

Trip counts are only part of the story. China spends nearly four times more than New Zealand despite similar trip volumes. Indian visitors stay the longest — averaging over 60 nights — largely due to visiting friends and family. Understanding spend and duration helps prioritise which markets to cultivate.

Section 03

When They Come & Why They Come

Visitor numbers are not constant throughout the year. Monthly data reveals strong seasonal peaks in Australian winter and a near-complete collapse during COVID-19 border closures. The recovery has been uneven — China has been slowest to return, while New Zealand and India rebounded fastest. Meanwhile, the purpose of travel has subtly shifted: holiday travel now makes up a smaller share than pre-pandemic, while education and visiting friends & relatives have grown in relative importance.

Section 04

Where in Australia Do They Go?

Once visitors arrive, their destinations are far from evenly distributed. New South Wales and Victoria capture the majority of arrivals, driven by Sydney and Melbourne's global appeal. Western Australia has recovered strongly beyond pre-COVID levels, while the Northern Territory is growing fastest — up 23.9% in a single year.

Section 05

Australia's Tourism Recovery

COVID-19 effectively shut down international tourism to Australia for two years. The path back has been uneven — short-term visitor arrivals still haven't fully recovered to 2019 levels nationally, while long-term arrivals have surpassed them.
